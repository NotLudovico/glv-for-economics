\begin{abstract}
Firm growth shows two robust anomalies. First, the volatility of a firm's growth rate
falls with size much more slowly than independence would predict: $\sigma(S)\sim
S^{-\beta}$ with $\beta\approx0.15$--$0.20$ rather than $1/2$. Second, the growth-rate
distribution is sharply peaked and fat-tailed rather than Gaussian. The standard
explanations place the source of these effects inside each firm, in a heavy-tailed
composition of internal sub-units. \citet{moran2024} test this granular picture against
firm data and find that it fails, and argue that the missing ingredient is correlations
that grow with a firm's size. We ask instead whether a dynamical model of interacting
firms can generate the same facts on its own, with no heterogeneity, noise, or
correlations imposed from outside, so that any size-dependent structure is produced by
the dynamics rather than assumed.

We model the economy as a Generalized Lotka--Volterra system on a sparse random graph
and study it in two stages. In the first the network is held at its cooperative critical
point $\mu_c$, where the restoring forces vanish and long-lived fluctuations survive.
This regime is hard to reach numerically, since the total abundance diverges in finite
time, so we split the dynamics into a bounded shape on the probability simplex and an
unbounded scale, and rescale time so the blow-up becomes smooth asymptotic growth; the
same construction locates $\mu_c$ cheaply. The critical dynamics reproduce the
fat-tailed growth-rate distribution on heterogeneous graphs, but the size--variance
relation is not a stationary law: it is a relaxation transient whose minimum is set by
the firms' initial sizes, so the static critical model offers no size--variance exponent
to compare with the data. Recovering that law calls for a genuinely nonstationary state,
one that keeps fluctuating rather than relaxing.

In the second stage we propose such a model. Letting the interactions act on the average
firm rather than on absolute abundances makes the dynamics scale-invariant: the economy
grows exponentially without ever blowing up, while in the disordered regime its
composition never settles but keeps churning. In this persistently fluctuating state the
two facts appear together as stationary properties, a symmetric fat-tailed growth
distribution and a size--variance relation that now falls with the empirical sign, and
they are robust across graph realizations, system size, and the sampling interval.
Switching off the disorder removes both, which locates their origin in the interactions
themselves rather than in any single-firm mechanism. Deterministic couplings between
firms can therefore generate the firm-growth anomalies endogenously, together with a
steadily growing economy; the size--variance exponent comes out with the right sign, and
its higher moments reproduce the anomalous multiscaling that the granular models cannot,
the signature of size-growing correlations, although the exponent's overall magnitude and
the near-critical character of the growth remain to be pinned down.
\end{abstract}

\chapter{Derivation of the rescaled equations}
\label{app:derivation}

We start from the Generalized Lotka--Volterra system,
\begin{equation}
    \dot{x}_i = x_i \left( 1 - x_i + \sum_j W_{ij} x_j \right),
    \label{eq:app-glv}
\end{equation}
and introduce the total abundance and relative fractions
\begin{equation}
    M = \sum_j x_j, \qquad y_i = \frac{x_i}{M}, \qquad x_i = M y_i .
\end{equation}
Throughout we use the scalar shorthands
\begin{equation}
    y^{\!\top} W y = \sum_{i,j} y_i W_{ij} y_j, \qquad
    y^{\!\top} y = \sum_i y_i^2 ,
\end{equation}
and the identities $\sum_i x_i^2 = M^2\, y^{\!\top} y$ and
$\sum_{i,j} x_i W_{ij} x_j = M^2\, y^{\!\top} W y$.

\section*{Dynamics of the total abundance}

Summing \eqref{eq:app-glv} over $i$,
\begin{align}
    \dot{M} &= \sum_i \dot{x}_i
            = \sum_i x_i - \sum_i x_i^2 + \sum_{i,j} x_i W_{ij} x_j \nonumber \\
            &= M - M^2\, y^{\!\top} y + M^2\, y^{\!\top} W y
            = M + M^2 \left( y^{\!\top} W y - y^{\!\top} y \right).
    \label{eq:app-Mdot}
\end{align}

\section*{Dynamics of the relative fractions}

Differentiating $y_i = x_i / M$ with the quotient rule,
\begin{equation}
    \dot{y}_i = \frac{\dot{x}_i}{M} - \frac{x_i \dot{M}}{M^2}
              = \frac{\dot{x}_i}{M} - y_i \frac{\dot{M}}{M}.
    \label{eq:app-ydot-quotient}
\end{equation}
The first term, using $x_i = M y_i$ in \eqref{eq:app-glv}, is
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\dot{x}_i}{M}
    = y_i \left( 1 - M y_i + M \sum_j W_{ij} y_j \right)
    = y_i \Big[ 1 + M\big( (Wy)_i - y_i \big) \Big].
\end{equation}
The second term, using \eqref{eq:app-Mdot}, is
\begin{equation}
    y_i \frac{\dot{M}}{M}
    = y_i \Big[ 1 + M\big( y^{\!\top} W y - y^{\!\top} y \big) \Big].
\end{equation}
Subtracting, the two ``$1$''s cancel and an overall factor $M$ survives,
\begin{equation}
    \dot{y}_i
    = M\, y_i \left[ (Wy)_i - y_i - y^{\!\top} W y + y^{\!\top} y \right].
    \label{eq:app-ydot}
\end{equation}

\section*{Rescaling time}

We finally pass to the stretched time $\tau$ defined by $d\tau/dt = M$, so that
$\dfrac{d}{d\tau} = \dfrac{1}{M}\dfrac{d}{dt}$. Applying this to
\eqref{eq:app-ydot} cancels the factor $M$,
\begin{equation}
    \frac{dy_i}{d\tau}
    = y_i \left[ (Wy)_i - y_i - y^{\!\top} W y + y^{\!\top} y \right],
\end{equation}
to \eqref{eq:app-Mdot} it gives
\begin{equation}
    \frac{dM}{d\tau} = \frac{\dot{M}}{M}
    = 1 + M \left( y^{\!\top} W y - y^{\!\top} y \right),
\end{equation}
and the physical time follows immediately by inverting the definition of $\tau$,
\begin{equation}
    \frac{dt}{d\tau} = \frac{1}{M}.
\end{equation}
These are equations \eqref{eq:dydtau}--\eqref{eq:dtdtau} of the main text. Note
that the $y$-equation no longer contains $M$, confirming that the simplex
dynamics is closed and insensitive to the divergence of the macroscopic
envelope.
