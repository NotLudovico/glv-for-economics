## 1. Framing decision (agreed)

**Spine: one question, two attempts.**

> **Question.** Moran–Bouchaud 2024 (MSB) show that granular / single-firm models fail the firm-growth data and
> argue the missing ingredient is *correlations that grow with firm size*. Can a **dynamical model of interacting
> firms** generate the stylized facts endogenously?
>
> **Attempt 1 — static GLV at criticality.** Reaching μ_c needs a dedicated numerical method (the rescaling).
> The critical dynamics give the heavy tails (on heterogeneous graphs) but the size–variance law is only a
> *relaxation transient*, not a stationary law. That failure diagnoses what is missing: a genuinely
> nonstationary, persistently fluctuating state.
>
> **Attempt 2 — relative GLV.** That state, made to grow without blow-up. It reproduces **both** facts, robustly
> (seeds/N/Δt), generated by the interactions (they vanish when the disorder is removed). **Answer: yes.**

### 1a. The contribution is a MODEL, not a correlation mechanism

We do **not** merely "supply the correlations MSB call for" — that would instantiate their hypothesis. We propose
a **first-principles dynamical model** whose stylized facts *emerge* with nothing injected. Contribution statement:

> A first-principles dynamical model of the economy — the relative Generalized Lotka–Volterra system — in which
> deterministic, disordered *inter-firm* interactions endogenously generate the firm-growth stylized facts
> (symmetric fat-tailed growth, volatility decaying with size) **together with** a steadily growing economy and
> persistent compositional churn, with no externally imposed noise, heterogeneity, or correlations.

It has teeth against the alternatives:
- **vs granular/sub-unit models:** they posit a fixed heavy-tailed internal composition; we posit none — the
  heterogeneity is generated by the network dynamics.
- **vs Bouchaud–Mézard:** growth + heavy tails there come from injected noise with no network; ours are
  deterministic and emergent from one ingredient (the disordered interactions; ablation proves it).
- **vs MSB's correlation story:** they hypothesize the mechanism; we give a dynamical system that produces the
  consequences, in which the mechanism can be measured rather than assumed.

### 1b. Honest scope boundary (keeps the bigger claim credible)

The model is shown to generate the **marginal** facts (growth-distribution shape + size–variance sign/trend). It
is **not yet** shown to reproduce MSB's **specific** size-dependent correlation *structure* — only its
consequences. So "we propose a dynamical model that generates the stylized facts" is defensible; "we explain the
size-dependent correlations" would overclaim. Measuring the emergent correlations directly is the headline
future-work item.

## 4. Chapter 3 (relative GLV) — expanded section plan

Sections (current → planned):
1. **Relative interactions and scale invariance** *(reframed)* — lead with the principle: an economy has no
   absolute size scale; the absolute GLV is not homogeneous (built-in carrying capacity), so it cannot grow
   without the interactions becoming pathological — the finite-time blow-up is the *symptom* of broken scale
   invariance. Dividing the regulation by m makes the dynamics homogeneous (invariant under x → cx); the shares
   obey a replicator equation (frequency-dependent / market-share competition). One symmetry buys (i) growing
   aggregate, (ii) stationary firm statistics, (iii) replicator competition. State the one caveat: the Malthusian
   rate sets a time scale, so only the *shape* dynamics are scale-free.
2. **Firm-growth statistics in the fluctuating state** — both facts on relative sizes S_i = N y_i; the empirical
   protocol; the MAD volatility estimator; decline-branch β with R² (plateau caveat).
